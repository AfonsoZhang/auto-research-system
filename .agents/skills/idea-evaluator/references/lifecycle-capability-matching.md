# Execution feasibility and resource matching

Use the [shared topic-selection policy](../../shared-references/topic-selection-policy.md).
Research categories do not have established expiry dates, GPU quotas or minimum
weekly hours. Estimate the current project, and separate competition risk from
contribution validity. Missing resources are a planning question, not proof that
an idea is unpublishable.

## Establish requirements before filtering by availability

1. Identify the question and the experiment/design that best answers it within
   explicit user constraints. Include essential comparisons, controls and access.
2. Distinguish preferred resources, minimally adequate alternatives and options
   that would change the claim. Do not quietly choose only what is currently local.
3. Record confirmed access, missing resources, acquisition options and costs.
   Ask about material cost/scope choices before relying on additional resources.
4. Estimate implementation, pilot, evaluation, analysis and writing effort from
   the actual task and known availability. Use ranges and name uncertainties;
   do not infer ability or publication probability from hours-per-week bands.
5. Identify the riskiest testable assumption and a bounded minimum decisive pilot.
   Use its predeclared continuation/stop criteria before larger spending.

## Timing and competition

Use actual deadlines, verified concurrent results and documented dependencies.
Someone else working nearby may affect positioning or scheduling; it does not
give the idea a fixed months-long expiry. Do not invent competitors' completion
times or require a months/years head start. Explain what could genuinely change
the contribution, and what will remain useful if similar work appears.

## Responses to a gap

- A needed resource may be obtainable: state the preferred choice and acquisition
  decision; continue independent work that remains useful and authorized.
- Access is unavailable now: PARK with a concrete resumption condition.
- A smaller design can answer the same question: explain why it remains adequate.
- A cheaper design changes the question: present it as a scope choice, not an
  equivalent substitute. Do not execute an unapproved change of direction.
- Reliable evidence defeats the current contribution: use the shared decision
  rules, considering any distinct negative finding rather than blaming resources.

Retain existing project formats, paths and frozen-version constraints. Planning
does not authorize paid runs, new infrastructure or automatic phase continuation.
