# Research strategy principles

Read the [shared topic-selection policy](../../shared-references/topic-selection-policy.md)
before making decisions. These principles support research planning; high-impact
aspirations do not replace the common contribution scale or four decisions.

Historical inspiration: Nicholas Carlini,
[How to Win a Best Paper Award](https://nicholas.carlini.com/writing/2026/how-to-win-a-best-paper-award.html).
Best-paper strategy is optional upside guidance, not a universal publication bar.
Official criteria and formally published calibration examples are in the shared policy.

## RS1: Establish the substantive delta

Ask what question, mechanism, setting, evidence or conclusion differs from the
closest verified work, and why it matters. A familiar combination or focused
increment can qualify. Competitive head start may inform scheduling but is neither
a measure of novelty nor a months/years prerequisite. Do not invent rivals' timelines.

## RS2: Explain the contribution's use

A hypothetical conclusion can clarify the intended scientific/practical consequence.
Mark it as hypothetical and include alternative outcomes when useful. A meaningful
efficiency gain, reliable measurement or negative finding may be enough in scope.
An unclear draft needs refinement; it does not establish that the project lacks value.

## RS3: Identify a coherent central question

Try to state the contribution concisely. Supporting claims can be multiple when
they form a coherent answer. A one-sentence nugget helps communication; failing
to write a compelling slogan is not a rejection test.

## RS4: Test the consequential uncertainty early

Identify the riskiest central assumption and design the smallest decisive pilot,
with support/refutation/inconclusive outcomes and bounded time/cost. Choose the
appropriate experiment before checking resource availability. Respect the existing
experiment-plan gate before scaling; no fixed one-week feasibility requirement.

## RS5: Stop or revise based on evidence

Do not continue a defeated claim because of sunk cost. Distinguish invalid
measurement, insufficient evidence and credible refutation; preserve meaningful
negative findings and failure boundaries. STOP_CURRENT_VERSION requires the shared
evidential condition, not low excitement, a crowded topic or incremental results.
PARK resource-blocked work with a resumption condition. Assess new scope explicitly.

## RS6: Make evidence sufficient for the claim

Run the relevant controls, uncertainty checks and coverage needed to substantiate
the actual conclusion. Extra experiments that do not affect it are optional.
Do not prescribe excessive effort, universal counts or unlimited validation loops.
Once agreed conditions are met, proceed within the authorized task or deliver.

## RS7: Use comparative advantage as a planning aid

Skills, data access, domain knowledge and cross-field insight help prioritize among
valuable options. Lacking a unique advantage over a large lab is not a veto. For
missing resources, compare acquisition/collaboration and adequate alternatives;
material scope/cost decisions belong with the user under the active task's rules.

## RS8: Track changing conditions

Use verified literature and actual deployment/venue changes to update importance,
priority wording and timing. Separate facts from forecasts. Mature or crowded
areas can still have valuable focused questions. A fixed expiry date cannot be
inferred from a field name or from the idea being incremental.
