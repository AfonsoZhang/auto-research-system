# Pre-Submission Reviewer

Use [paper-type-routing.md](../shared-references/paper-type-routing.md) to select
applicable checks. For scope/claim corrections, use
[claim-preservation.md](../shared-references/claim-preservation.md): identify the
unsupported assertion, the smallest justified correction and the contribution
that remains. Reuse supplied revision history without repeatedly weakening a
resolved claim. This review does not automatically rewrite or start another phase.

## Scope and route

Review the requested manuscript or sections. Read existing contributions,
evidence and author constraints; establish contribution type, stage and target
venue separately. Mixed papers get secondary checks only for their actual claims.
Read just the relevant profile in the shared route; do not apply all profiles.
Infer a provisional type when clear; clarify only an ambiguity that changes the
assessment. No new intake form is required.

A paragraph proofread is not a full submission verdict. A proposal can be assessed
for question and evidence-plan quality without pretending it has finished results.
Use current official venue/track rules for a requested readiness decision; unknown
requirements remain unverified. State a demonstrated scope mismatch and its source
without treating it as evidence that the research has no value.

For other disciplines, interpret evidence according to the research method:
proofs for formal claims, sources and arguments for humanities, identification
and sampling for causal/observational work. Do not automatically apply a CS
method-paper scaffold because the topic involves AI or software.

## Review procedure

### 1. Contribution, logic and evidence

Use the applicable profile and references/logic-and-structure.md.

- Can the reader identify the question, contribution, relevant prior work and
  what the evidence establishes? Arguments can be organized by findings, proofs,
  design decisions, resources or synthesis, not only methodology modules.
- Does each substantive claim have the right support: results, proof, validated
  measurement or sourced reasoning? Check whether necessary comparisons/controls
  are missing, explaining which claim depends on them.
- Require component attribution controls when a causal/mechanistic claim needs
  them. Descriptive observations do not acquire a causal claim by being useful.
- Check scope and warranted strength in both directions. A claim of “first”,
  “best” or “solves” needs appropriate support; the word itself is not a defect.
- If a running example, architecture figure or module mapping is used, check
  consistency and relevance. Absence alone is not a submission blocker.

Do not impose fixed counts of datasets, contributions, modules or experiments.
Assess uncertainty and coverage where they affect the actual conclusion. A small,
focused study can be adequate for its claim; a type label does not excuse weak
evidence or rescue a refuted assertion.

When literature verification is within the requested review, use the installed
literature workflow and [publication policy](../shared-references/publication-policy.md).
Compare the closest relevant works on claims and evidence, not titles alone.
Metadata can verify a reference's identity; it cannot establish method-level
duplication. Retrieve the needed passages before making that judgment. Explain
how an omitted reference or baseline changes positioning or validity; not every
omission is MAJOR. Retrieval failure remains an uncertainty, not a novelty verdict.

### 2. Writing and section structure

Use references/section-guides.md for adaptable method-paper examples. Judge other
types by their selected argument: measurement/design, empirical findings, proof,
replication, synthesis or thesis. Keep an adequate existing structure.

Check that paragraphs have a clear function, transitions connect ideas, terms
are stable and repetition serves a purpose. Paragraph counts, sentence counts,
six-part introductions and one-to-one module/section mappings are not universal
requirements. Abstracts state the actual contribution and supported outcome;
theory or position papers do not need invented experimental numbers.

Use [abstract and findings guidance](../shared-references/abstract-and-findings.md)
for abstract emphasis, informative conclusions and specific limitations. Do not
require a limitations sentence in the abstract or a counterintuitive main result.

For Limitations, apply the [admission gate](../limitation-and-risk-writer/SKILL.md#admission-gate-all-four-conditions-required)
against the current body. Identify semantic duplicates and generic truisms for
deletion or consolidation, not cosmetic rewording. Do not demand an additional
limitations paragraph just because an optional section is empty; material
disclosures already adequately explained in the body satisfy disclosure, subject
to explicit venue/user requirements. A missing source is an author query, and an
unsupported central claim requires a body correction rather than a generic caveat.
Check the measured word count and distinct-point cap under the
[Limitations length budget](../limitation-and-risk-writer/SKILL.md#length-budget-hard-acceptance-limits).
An unexplained excess is a writing correction, not a scientific rejection criterion;
no minimum length or point count is required. Respect explicit documented overrides.

### 3. Grammar and academic prose

Consult references/grammar-rules.md for actual grammar problems and
[academic prose guidance](../paper-polish/references/ai-tone-guardrails.md) for
clarity and fidelity. Treat references/forbidden-patterns.md as contextual warning
signs, not a word blacklist. Preserve technical terms, meaningful punctuation,
supported comparisons and uncertainty. Repeated “superior” may merit checking its
evidence; counting it does not establish either overclaim or AI authorship.

Apply the [direct-claim acceptance rules](../paper-polish/references/ai-tone-guardrails.md#state-supported-claims-directly)
to defensive framing. Recommend the smallest edit that preserves the supported
claim, actual uncertainty and required disclosures. Stylistic caution by itself
is not a reason to weaken a result or assign scientific severity.

Apply the shared author's dash restriction within its stated scope and
exceptions. Report violations as presentation corrections, normally MINOR;
do not turn punctuation counts into scientific criticism or MAJOR findings.

### 4. Format and figures

Use the actual project/venue template and references/latex-rules.md for applicable
LaTeX checks. Check compilation/cross-references when tools and scope permit,
citations, legibility, captions and verified page/anonymity requirements.
Keep project paths, labels and file organization unless an actual problem needs
repair. No LaTeX defect can be inferred from an unavailable source file.

Prefer vector plots/diagrams; adequate-resolution raster evidence is appropriate
for photographs, screenshots or image data. Check readable size, accurate axes,
accessible encoding and captions matched to the figure's role. Neither raster
format alone nor a missing architecture diagram is a universal serious defect.

### 5. Reconcile and deliver

Reuse evidence/concern locators to deduplicate findings across dimensions and
previous supplied rounds. Distinguish required corrections, unresolved checks
and optional suggestions. Compare any material narrowing with the justified
reference claim on the author side; keep the remaining contribution visible.
Do not add an independent model review as a prerequisite to this skill.

## Severity

- **CRITICAL:** a substantiated flaw invalidates the central claimed conclusion,
  or a verified mandatory submission rule is violated. Explain the actual impact
  and evidence; an incomplete assessment cannot be called a proven flaw.
- **MAJOR:** a material gap in evidence, positioning, reproducibility or clarity
  affects an asserted contribution and needs resolution. Identify that dependency.
- **MINOR:** a local correctness or presentation issue with limited impact.
- **Optional / N/A:** an extension, taste preference or inapplicable template
  check; do not count it as a scientific weakness or submission blocker.

Unresolved justified blockers preclude a ready verdict. Readiness otherwise
depends on what the remaining issues affect, not fixed counts of MAJOR findings.
Do not assign a numeric acceptance probability. If the user requests a rubric
score, use the selected venue's verified rubric and explain the score; it does
not replace the assessment or the shared topic-selection decision.

## Integrity and output

Before delivery, check that each finding has a locator, an accurate quotation or
paraphrase, the applicable claim/rule and a proportional suggested correction.
Do not fabricate missing evidence, quotes or completed checks. Scope and severity
must match the actual inspected material. A partial scan cannot be reported as
a full-paper review. Resolve these checks internally, without a ritual checklist
or an additional user approval for ordinary review work.

For a full review, use a brief summary of contribution/type, review coverage and
the important findings, followed by a compact table:

| Finding and locator | Affected claim/rule | Severity | Suggested correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Specific observed issue | Why it matters here | CRITICAL / MAJOR / MINOR | Smallest justified correction |

Put optional suggestions separately and retain supported strengths. A submission
recommendation is appropriate only when requested and the evidence/venue checks
support it; otherwise state what remains unverified. For a small edit or proofread,
deliver only the relevant findings rather than the full review template.
