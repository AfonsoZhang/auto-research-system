# Preserve the contribution across revisions

Use [paper-type-routing.md](paper-type-routing.md) when selecting structure or evidence requirements. Preserve the agreed contribution type unless a substantive, justified change is within scope; a failed method result must not silently become a different paper merely to clear a checklist.

Local writing/analysis policy. Read when a task
creates, removes, narrows, strengthens or reframes a substantive research claim.
It applies to analysis, story, outline, prose, Limitations and review-driven edits.
For language-only edits, preserve meaning; no history audit or new report is needed.

When a skill routes here, compare with the existing question, justified contribution
and evidence; justify any narrowing and reassess the remaining value. Reuse project
records and keep ordinary language edits lightweight. Applying this policy does not
authorize a new phase or review.

When expressing central findings or placing qualifications in the paper, also use
[abstract-and-findings.md](abstract-and-findings.md). Preserve informative supported
claims; neither a generic truism nor an invented surprise repairs weak evidence.

Aim for the strongest useful contribution that the evidence supports. Check both
overstatement and unnecessary weakening. Earlier wording and author agreement
are reference points, not proof: correct real errors even if they were accepted
before. Do not retain an unsupported claim just to keep a paper broad.

## Establish the reference claim

Reuse the existing outline, manuscript, analysis report, Evidence Map or change
record. Identify the current research question, central contribution, supported
claim and evidence locators. When available, compare with the last substantively
justified or author-agreed version as well as the immediately preceding version;
this exposes cumulative changes hidden by a sequence of small edits.

Keep distinct:

- **Question and significance:** what uncertainty the work addresses and whose
  understanding, measurement or design decision it could change.
- **Evidence coverage:** tested tasks, models, data, conditions and comparisons.
- **Inference:** what observation, effect, mechanism or generality that design
  supports, and which broader implications are reasoned but not demonstrated.

A bounded experiment can answer a useful question. Limited coverage does not by
itself erase the question's significance or establish failure elsewhere. Neither
an appealing implication nor a broad introduction proves untested generality.

If history or underlying evidence is unavailable, state that limit. Do not invent
an original claim, assert that cumulative drift was checked, or narrow a claim
merely because the needed context was not supplied. Use the available evidence
and identify the specific missing item only when it affects the requested decision.

## Check each substantive change in both directions

Before deleting or narrowing a supported claim, explain the particular reason:
new results, a newly identified inference/measurement error, verified prior-work
coverage, an actual design boundary, or an explicit author decision. New experiments
are not required to discover an old error. Reviewer preference, a generic possible
limitation, small dataset count or cautious-sounding wording is not enough.

Compare the reference, current and proposed claim along the affected dimensions:
population/setting, outcome, mechanism/causality, certainty, priority or usefulness.
Change only what the evidence requires and retain independently supported parts.
Do not turn uncertainty about one mechanism into uncertainty about an observed
effect, or remove an entire contribution because a broader generalization failed.

Reuse the same evidence/concern locator across handoffs. If a limitation is already
adequately accounted for, do not downgrade the claim again for that same reason.
A later pass may correct an inadequate earlier fix, but must identify the remaining
unsupported assertion or new basis. This is not a fixed limit on revision rounds.

Check for lost warranted strength too: deletion of a supported contribution,
unjustified "merely a case study/preliminary observation" labels, stacked qualifiers
that change meaning, or loss of the practical/scientific consequence. Restore or
propose the evidence-supported wording within the task's authorization. Pure
polishing must not silently strengthen claims either.

Do not repair a failed broad result by repeatedly selecting a favorable subset.
Retain the original result/coverage, disclose post hoc restrictions and label
exploratory findings. A real boundary-condition result can be valuable without
being recast as the original successful prediction.

## After a material narrowing, reassess the remaining contribution

Ask whether the revised result still answers the stated question and contributes
something distinguishable and useful. Name what a reader can now understand,
measure or decide. A focused mechanism, informative replication, negative result
or failure boundary can qualify. No minimum breadth, effect size, model count or
mandatory high-impact framing applies; use [topic-selection-policy.md](topic-selection-policy.md).

If it does, retain that contribution and check that affected title, abstract,
Introduction, contribution list, Discussion and Limitations tell a consistent
story within the authorized editing scope. Flag edits outside that scope.

If the remaining claim no longer answers the question or its value is unresolved,
state the gap rather than declaring success because every sentence is cautious.
Present the evidence-based options that apply: keep an independently supported
contribution, obtain decisive evidence, explicitly revise the research question,
PARK for a prerequisite, or STOP_CURRENT_VERSION when the shared stop condition
holds. Do not terminate merely because the result is narrow. Do not silently
replace the research question, add experiments or continue shrinking indefinitely.
Material direction/scope/cost decisions follow the user's existing authorization
and the active workspace mode; this rule adds no approval step for ordinary edits.

## Carry the comparison forward without adding a new bureaucracy

Use the existing Evidence Map, analysis report, revision note or requested diff.
For a substantive change retain enough to identify: the reference/current/proposed
claim, evidence or concern locator, reason, resolved versus open issue, and the
remaining scientific/practical contribution. A short note may suffice. Preserve
existing IDs, schemas, paths and project boundaries; do not require a new ledger,
folder, automatic snapshot, report or full-document review for a small edit.

Carry the justified claim and already-accounted-for limitations into the next
authorized handoff, so the next skill does not treat every caveat as new evidence.
Keep this reasoning outside manuscript prose unless scientifically relevant. A
recorded decision is not evidence: later agents must inspect sources when needed.

## Review and audit boundary

For reviews, distinguish necessary corrections from optional extensions and state
the smallest justified change, including the supported contribution that remains.
When revision history is within the review's inputs, use it to detect repeated
weakening; when not, do not pretend to assess cumulative drift.

A zero-context paper-to-raw-evidence audit keeps its independent inputs. Do not
send author narratives, previous verdicts or change records to that auditor. It
can locate the actual scope mismatch and avoid blanket narrowing. The author-side
revision step applies the cross-version comparison afterward. This policy does
not add mandatory reviewer/model calls or change existing audit result schemas.

When applying an audit's scope finding, correct to the exact verified scope with
its count rather than a blanket hedge: "97 of 103 tasks need several tools" for
"every task needs several", "none of the compared structures" for "no structure".
Re-check every location carrying the same claim (abstract, Introduction,
Results, Conclusion). Fix only the severity the user asked for; list the rest.

## Boundary examples (hypothetical)

- Tested English tasks: qualify untested multilingual transfer; retain the supported
  English result and any justified design implication. Do not infer poor transfer.
- The same transfer caveat returns in another round: check whether the present
  wording already addresses it before adding further restrictions.
- A valid control reveals a confound: revise the causal attribution even without
  a new experiment; assess the independently supported observation separately.
- Repeated narrowing leaves an uninformative restatement of one configuration:
  report the unanswered question and options instead of treating polished prose
  as evidence of an adequate paper.
