# Idea Evaluator

Apply the shared [working-language policy](../shared-references/research-autonomy.md#working-language) to internal work and user reports.

Evaluate whether an idea is worth developing, what remains uncertain and which
next step would resolve it. Read the
[shared topic-selection policy](../shared-references/topic-selection-policy.md)
first. It governs contribution scale, evidence, resources and the four decisions;
this skill does not simulate an acceptance decision from a top-venue reviewer.

## Scope

Use for a draft idea, candidate comparison, feasibility check or scope revision.
For a small revision, read the existing evaluation and update affected judgments
only. An existing paper review or writing task stays with the applicable writing
skill; it does not restart topic selection. Brainstorming from scratch can use
the installed brainstorming skills if within the requested task.

## Procedure

### 1. Clarify the contribution

Restate the question, intended contribution and scientific/practical use. Include
mechanism improvements, combinations, new settings, evaluation/resources,
systematic analysis, failure boundaries, negative findings and informative
replication. If the claim is vague, propose a precise interpretation and identify
the missing detail; do not infer that an unclear pitch is an invalid idea.
Apply [question value and hypothesis relationships](../shared-references/topic-selection-policy.md#question-value-and-hypothesis-relationships)
to distinguish a substantive attribution question from a supporting ablation,
and competing explanations from mechanisms that can coexist or form a causal chain.

Use the actual research method to choose an assessment framework. For non-STEM
work, consult `references/domain-evaluation-frameworks.md`; retain its relevant
methodological checks, but use the shared evidence/decision rules rather than
any inherited score or venue heuristic as an automatic rejection threshold.

### 2. Establish the delta and investigate blockers

Read `references/fatal-flaws.md` as a checklist of possible problems. Record the
important specific issues, evidence status and possible resolution. Do not turn
their count into a decision or infer a fatal problem from missing information.

Ground novelty judgments in actual retrieval. Use relevant keyword groups and
follow closest work; reuse the publication records governed by
[publication-policy.md](../shared-references/publication-policy.md). Read the
relevant passages before judging method/result overlap. A different title or
one differing axis alone is not enough: identify the meaningful added knowledge
or capability. Report limited retrieval as limited retrieval, not proof of novelty.

Separate invalid measurements, underpowered/inconclusive results and credible
refutation. A baseline tie/loss alone cannot force rejection. Reliable evidence
against a claimed mechanism requires revising that claim, followed by a separate
assessment of any valuable negative finding or other remaining contribution.

### 3. Assess value and resources separately

Explain who can use the contribution and what decision or understanding it changes.
A narrow scientific audience is legitimate. For method ideas, optionally use the
Higher/Faster/Stronger/Cheaper/Broader lenses in `references/five-dimensions.md`.
They are not required achievements for an audit, replication or negative result.
Do not require two high scores, SOTA, a minimum speedup or broad generality.

Read `references/lifecycle-capability-matching.md` for resource planning. Specify
the scientifically appropriate experiment first, identify the user's actual
resources and gaps, and recommend how to acquire missing essentials or compare
valid alternatives. Ask about material scope/cost choices. Do not constrain the
research to the equipment currently available or infer capability from a category.

### 4. Identify the smallest decisive validation

For a central uncertainty, state the claim, relevant baseline/control, observations
that would support/refute it, an inconclusive outcome and the budget/stop condition.
Reuse existing experiment-plan artifacts. Do not automatically run the experiment.
Select replication and coverage according to the claim, not fixed counts. A pilot
can justify stopping expenditure or revising the claim without rejecting a field.

### 5. Optional strategic upside

Use `references/paradigm-shift-probe.md` when broader strategic exploration is
useful or requested. Disruptive potential, competitive head start and novelty of
framing are bonuses. Their absence never blocks a focused contribution. A draft
conclusion is a hypothesis about what the work could establish, not invented data.

### 6. Decide

Apply the shared four decisions: **CONTINUE**, **VALIDATE_FIRST**,
**PARK**, **STOP_CURRENT_VERSION**. State the evidential
reason and next action; do not translate scores or issue counts into verdicts.
STOP_CURRENT_VERSION applies to the present claim/scope and needs the exact
covered/refuted claim plus consideration of worthwhile remaining contributions.

## Delivery

Scale the output to the request. A full evaluation may use:

1. Question, contribution type and candidate delta.
2. Closest works: publication/version status, compared claim, evidence locator,
   meaningful difference or unresolved comparison.
3. Research value, evidence strength and important limitations, separated.
4. Preferred experiment/resources, actual access and missing prerequisites.
5. Common decision and its reason.
6. Next decisive action and its continuation/stop/resumption conditions.

Optional dimension ratings must distinguish measured evidence, mechanism-based
expectation and unknown/not-applicable. They are not acceptance probabilities or
gates. `references/worked-examples.md` illustrates field calibration and edge cases.

Before delivery, check that negative judgments are sourced, unknowns stay unknown,
attribution claims stay within what the comparisons identify, resource claims reflect known
facts, and the decision follows the shared policy. Fix errors in the evaluation
instead of punishing the research idea for a reporting error. Complete this call
without automatically starting another skill, review, team or experiment.
